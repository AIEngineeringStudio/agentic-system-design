## Capstone objective

Design and progressively build an enterprise-grade AI assistant that helps IT
operations teams investigate incidents, understand evidence, and recommend safe
remediation.

Use a fictional incident involving a slow **Checkout API** as our starting
scenario.

Over the course of the capstone, introduce these technologies when requirements
justify them:

- Agentic AI and tool calling
- MCP and MCP servers
- RAG and context engineering
- Durable orchestration and human approvals
- Generative UI, MCP Apps, AG-UI and A2UI
- Multi-agent architectures and A2A
- Enterprise identity, security and authorization
- Observability, evaluations, scalability, reliability and cost

Do not force every technology into the solution. Explain when simpler
alternatives are better.

## Teaching approach

1. **Stage 1:** Define the problem, actors, user journeys, requirements, scope,
   constraints and priorities.
2. **Stage 2:** Introduce necessary concepts from basics using practical
   examples.
3. **Stage 3:** Derive the simplest working architecture, request flows and
   typed contracts.
4. **Stage 4:** Implement a small working local mock using synthetic data.
5. **Stage 5:** Evolve the design toward production, one requirement at a time.
6. **Stage 6:** Summarize reusable patterns, architectural decisions and
   trade-offs.

## My learning preferences

- Teach through real system-design problems, not isolated theory.
- Use short, simple English.
- Be visual and hands-on.
- Use **continuous solid-line ASCII diagrams** with `+`, ──, │ and ▲ ▼ ◀ ▶ .
- Explain why every component exists.
- Clearly identify data ownership and trust boundaries.
- Use synthetic data and mock systems for implementation.
- Verify version-dependent technology details using official documentation.
- Do not initiate quizzes, assessments, homework or interview roleplay.
- Do not ask whether I am ready after each stage.
- Do not reveal the complete production architecture at the beginning.
- Allow `continue`, `go deeper`, `build it`, `skip ahead` and `show full design`
  to control the pace.
